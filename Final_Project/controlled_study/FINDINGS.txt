CONTROLLED LEVEL-CHANGE STUDY: RESULTS AND INTERPRETATION

All 36 planned mixtures and 144 method rows completed. These are six reused
sentences, with car/street noise averaged within each sentence. Results are
descriptive paired effects; no independent36-case or overlapping-frame
inference, no significance claim, and no new-speaker validation is implied.

1. The selected setting remains a trade-off, not a universally superior
method. Its whole-record STOI is slightly above original VAD and fixed in all
three profile means, but rising-noise SNR is below both. In the falling
profile, even selected VAD STOI remains below the unprocessed mixture
(0.905909 versus 0.907051), despite greater reference SNR.

2. The controlled change provides a clearer answer to WHEN updating helps.
After the ramp plus100 ms guard, both VAD methods perform zero updates in
all 12 rising-noise cases. They label every such valid frame as speech,
including 304 clean-energy non-speech-proxy frames. Their mean late estimate
to injected-noise power ratios are -8.575 dB (original) and -8.675 dB
(selected); the fixed estimator is -8.527 dB. The VAD's ratio test can close
the noise-update gate when noise rises, so adaptation cannot catch up in
this experiment. Gate closure and underestimation are directly visible in
the traces; the broadband ratio is a diagnostic, not a spectral PSD score.

3. Falling noise opens more update opportunities. Mean late update fractions
are 34.665% original VAD and 17.597% selected VAD, versus 23.505% and 1.431% in
the constant-added-gain profile. Falling-profile mean late tracking ratios
are +2.178 dB original, +2.642 dB selected and +3.473 dB fixed. Updating
reduces broadband overestimation here. Selected-fixed late SNR improves
0.394906 dB. Compared with its constant-profile contrast, that paired
late-SNR advantage increases 0.339688 dB; for rising noise it decreases
0.113026 dB. This manipulation supports a directional adaptation limitation
for these samples and settings, not general superiority of one method.

4. Sentence variation is shown, not hidden. Selected-fixed falling-profile
whole SNR is positive for all 6 sentence units (0.054825 to 0.293875 dB), and
STOI is also positive for all 6 (0.000349 to 0.004096). Rising-profile SNR
ranges -0.272107 to +0.049949 dB, and STOI -0.000803 to +0.003384. The negative
rising mean SNR is driven largely by sp27; sp17/sp22 are identical to fixed
under the selected gate. Do not convert these tiny descriptive STOI
differences into claims of audible improvement or word-recognition gains.

5. The preselected sp02/street/rising plot is an explanatory failure case,
not a handpicked success: in its 109 late frames, both VADs update 0 times;
27 frames meet the non-speech energy proxy. The plot shows estimate used
before update, windowed actual injected-noise power, gates and update marks.
Speech proxies are not manual annotations. The constant-gain noise already
varies naturally; only the imposed level change is controlled.

LIMITATIONS AND NEXT STEP
This small within-realization intervention changes amplitude, not noise
spectrum/type. It uses previous test sentences and provides diagnostic rather
than independent predictive evidence. The initial 100 ms contains small clean
leakage (worst clean-to-residual ratio -28.2625 dB), identical across matched
profiles/methods. Existing threshold/hangover values were not optimized for
these remixes. A future independent study could test a speech-presence-aware
or minimum-statistics noise estimator that can recover from upward shifts;
that is an untested proposal, not an implemented result. No listening study
has been conducted for these outcomes.
